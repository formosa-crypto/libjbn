Worth wiring into a field only for 64 limbs and more, or where BMI2/ADX cannot be required; it has no squaring;
  - a squaring of their own for the looped modules (they square by multiplying): the largest gain left from 32 limbs on. The unrolled squaring of `bn_mulx` alone is 39% faster than the looped multiplication at 32 limbs (199 against 325 ns). Until it exists, a 32-limb field could take `fun_sqrn` from a `bn_mulx` instance (19 KB, which still fits the instruction cache; 42 KB at 48 limbs, 75 KB at 64) and the rest from `bn_mulx_loop`: `fp_ctx_mulx_sqru7.jazz` is that wiring (tested in the interpreter; in `make run-mulx`), and `make bench` times it as the variant `mulx_sqru` (`BENCH_VARIANTS="mulx_unr mulx mulx_sqru" BENCH_SIZES="24 32 40 48"`). [Done] timed on the i7-14700K, one run (README, "The unrolled squaring in a looped instance"): from 24 to 40 limbs `fpc_sqr` is 19% to 21% faster than with the looped squaring and `fpc_exp` 14% to 17% (1 495 against 1 753 µs at 32 limbs, as projected); at 48 limbs, where the squaring has 42 KB, both are 4% slower. [Done] the selector does it: its `_sqrn_` is unrolled up to `JBN_SQR_UNROLL_MAX`, a threshold of the profile of its own (40 in `x86_64_adx.jinc`; 64 in `x86_64.jinc`, as its `JBN_UNROLL_MAX`: not timed apart without MULX); a scope that shadows `JBN_UNROLL_MAX` for the smallest code now has to shadow both. Still to do: sizes between 40 and 48, and between 16 and 24, where the multiplication crosses over (at 24 limbs the looped one is 11% faster, at 16 the unrolled one 4%). Above that size, two designs, neither tried: bands of rows (one unrolled row per band of 8, looped over the rows of the band on a zero-padded operand), or a split over the half size (two unrolled half squarings and a looped half multiplication, a module of two sizes as `bn_karatsuba`). A run-time loop over the products of a row is not one: a carry can live across a loop counted by `DEC`, but only one, and the rows use two. In GMP built for the same CPU the squaring alone takes 0.56 to 0.59 of the multiplication from 16 limbs on (`mpn_sec_sqr` 222.8 ns, `mpn_sec_mul` 385.2 at 32 limbs; `make bench-gmp` in `fp_ctx`), and the windowed exponentiation (`_expmW_`, now `fpc_exp`; below, "conditional operations") is mostly squarings: four products of five. At 32 limbs it is level with `mpz_powm_sec` (1 758 against 1 775 µs); with the 125 ns that the unrolled squaring saves on each `fpc_sqr`, it would take about 1 500 µs (a projection);
  - run-time loop over the limbs of the exponent in `_expmML`/`_expm_noct` (now the largest routines of a looped instance: 16.0 and 12.6 KB at 64 limbs). The windowed `_expmW` (below, "conditional operations") has such a loop: 2.5 KB at every size, lookup included.
